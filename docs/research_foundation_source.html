Problem in one line. When an Indian shopper asks an AI assistant for beauty products, does it name and link Sephora India, and how does that compare with Nykaa? Sephora India's digital team needs this to decide which content and product-feed fixes to make first.
This document answers four questions that come before the analysis: (1) which AI engines we study and why, (2) what earlier research and industry work says, (3) what outside data tells us about the market, and (4) how our data was collected and why it can support the analysis.

1. AI engine benchmarking: why these five engines

Selection rule. An engine enters the panel if it meets all three tests: (a) real reach among Indian users, judged from at least two independent sources; (b) it answers beauty-shopping questions with live web retrieval or visible sources, because our outcomes are retailer names and links; (c) our team can query it repeatably through a normal public account.

2. Literature review

Review question. What do research and official guidance establish about visibility in AI-generated answers, how can it be measured, and what can public observations tell us about Sephora India versus Nykaa?

2.1 Generative engine optimization (GEO) and measuring visibility

Aggarwal et al. (2024) define GEO as improving how often and how prominently content appears in answers from generative search engines, and introduce GEO-bench, a benchmark of queries across domains. They report visibility gains of up to 40% for some strategies in their experiments, with effects that vary by domain. What it gives us: visibility can be defined and measured, and strategies can be tested. Limit: the gains were found in the authors' setting and should not be assumed for Sephora India, today's products, or revenue. A critical survey of 45 studies (Martinez, 2026, preprint; we have not checked its publication status) frames GEO as a multi-stage, partly observable process (discovery, retrieval, ranking, citation, prominence) with inconsistent metrics and little evidence of stable cross-platform effects on real demand. What it gives us: a reason to report several metrics (mention, link, position) and to repeat runs.

2.2 How AI shopping agents choose products

Allouah et al. (2025) built ACES, a sandbox where AI agents pick products from a mock store. Different models produced very different selection shares for the same products; position effects were large, differed by provider and model version, and could reverse between versions; a "Sponsored" tag lowered selection while a platform "Overall Pick" tag raised it; telling agents to ignore position did not remove the effect. Microsoft Research's Magentic Marketplace (2025) found that frontier agents approach optimal outcomes only under ideal search conditions, degrade as the number of options grows, and show a strong first-proposal bias. What it gives us: engine and model version matter (our H3, engine control), position inside an answer matters (the position feature), and results can shift when a model is updated (a reason to record dates and model labels).

2.3 Official platform guidance

Google Search Central (2026) says foundational SEO still matters for Google's AI features, stresses useful, original and reliable content and technical eligibility, warns against unsupported GEO "hacks", and notes that inclusion is not guaranteed. Limit: this is Google guidance for Google Search only, not proof about ChatGPT or Gemini apps.

2.4 Sephora, Nykaa and AI commerce

  • Sephora (24 March 2026) announced a Sephora app in ChatGPT for curated advice and product discovery, first piloted in the United States. This shows AI commerce activity, but not Sephora India's visibility.
  • Reliance Industries (3 November 2023) announced the Sephora tie-up with Reliance Retail Ventures to build Sephora's India presence: business context, not evidence about AI visibility.
  • Nykaa and OpenAI signed a multi-year partnership (reported 17 June 2026) that lists Nykaa's beauty and fashion storefronts as connected apps in ChatGPT, drawing on Nykaa's catalogue and routing shoppers to Nykaa's app or website [5]. What it gives us: a plausible reason for Nykaa to be strong in ChatGPT, and a reason to check whether its lead also appears on engines outside this partnership (our data, section 3.2).

2.5 Industry measurement examples

Cited Index (2026), Best Skincare & Beauty in AI Search India, August 2026, uses non-branded consumer prompts across five AI platforms and publishes mention, position and citation measures. AccessAegis (2026), AI Visibility in India: 2026 Brand Study, describes 15 buyer prompts per brand on ChatGPT and Perplexity with saved answers and URLs. Both are vendor work: useful as protocol examples, not as neutral evidence, and neither (as described) estimates repeat-run variation.

3.2 What our own data adds

The Nykaa partnership is with OpenAI, so one might expect Nykaa's lead to be a ChatGPT effect. In our 222 unbranded answers, Nykaa is named more often than Sephora India on all five engines (ChatGPT 61% vs 22%, Perplexity 53% vs 11%, Claude 30% vs 9%, Gemini 12% vs 5%, Google AI Mode 10% vs 3%). The partnership may help in ChatGPT, but it does not by itself explain the gap on engines outside it. This is an observation, not a causal finding.

4. Data architecture

The design goal: every number in the analysis can be traced back to one question, one engine, one collector, one date and one saved answer.

1. Design20 English shopper questions in 7 categories; question types: need, budget, shortlist, where-to-buy; 1 branded comparison kept apart
→
2. Schedule600 planned runs, engine mix from the benchmark, runs interleaved so every engine gets every question; each run assigned a collector
→
3. CollectionQuestion typed into the engine; full answer text and source links copied into the shared sheet with date, model label and collection method
→
4. CleaningSheet merged to one CSV; checker tests required fields, duplicates, valid prompt IDs and engine names
→
5. FeaturesText rules: retailer named, retailer linked, position in answer, cited sites
→
6. AnalysisEDA and tests (D3), models and simulator (D4 onward)

4.4 Quality controls, versioning and responsible use

  • Controls: a validation script checks required fields, run IDs, prompt IDs, engines and status values; duplicate answers are removed by text hash; representativeness (planned vs collected engine mix) and collector and method bias are re-checked on the cleaned data in the notebook.
  • Versioning: each data release is tagged (this one is data-v0.9); the notebook reads the same CSV files that are in the submission, so restart-and-run-all reproduces every figure.
  • Not collected: synthetic or reference text that is not a real engine output is never loaded into the log.
  • Privacy: questions are generic shopper questions; no personal data is stored. Accounts were our own.
  • Platform terms: the 40 Gemini API answers were collected through the API rather than the app; this is recorded in the collection method so it can be analysed or removed. Terms of the engines and of sephora.in and nykaa.com are being reviewed for the full collection.
  • AI-use disclosure: Claude was used to build the sheet, scripts and notebook and to draft text; the team reviewed the outputs.
