2. Project charter

Problem statement

Businesses increasingly need to understand how generative AI platforms discover, describe and recommend their brands and products. However, they often lack an integrated intelligence system to measure AI-generated brand visibility, evaluate product representation and source credibility, identify discoverability opportunities, and translate findings into actionable content improvements.

BEYOND THE SEARCH BAR aims to develop an AI-powered, agentic GEO Intelligence Platform that integrates AI discovery monitoring, visibility diagnostics, opportunity identification, content optimisation, experimentation and autonomous workflow orchestration to help businesses improve their representation in AI-generated answers.

Charter elementDefinition
PurposeBuild a configurable platform that measures how selected AI systems represent a brand, diagnoses observable issues and opportunities, recommends evidence-grounded actions and helps evaluate changes.
Primary usersBrand managers, digital marketing teams, e-commerce teams, SEO/GEO specialists and strategy teams.
First use caseAssess beauty and skincare discovery queries in India for Sephora India and compare observations with Nykaa and Tira.
Core valueConnect discovery measurement to diagnosis, recommendations, content action and experimentation in one workflow.
Expected capstone outcomeWorking integrated prototype, documented data/AI pipeline, evaluation evidence, agent workflow, business recommendations, literature review and final academic submissions.
Success criteriaA user can configure a brand assessment, collect traceable AI responses, inspect visibility/accuracy/source signals, receive evidence-linked opportunities and content suggestions, and run or compare a repeat/variant assessment.
Evidence boundaries: We do not assume Sephora has a visibility gap, do not claim company collaboration, and cannot inspect private AI ranking algorithms. Public-prompt observations are a sample, not the whole market. Before/after changes are not causal proof unless the experiment design supports that conclusion.

3. The six product engines

1. AI Discovery Intelligence Engine

Manages the prompt library, selected AI providers, assessment runs, response collection, metadata and citation capture. Creates the evidence base for all downstream modules.

Prompt orchestrationProvider APIsResponse capture

2. AI Visibility Diagnostic Engine

Measures brand/product mentions, recommendation position, category coverage, competitor presence, product-detail accuracy and source/citation patterns. Flags observable gaps and inconsistencies.

NLPMetricsBenchmarking

3. GEO Opportunity & Recommendation Engine

Turns diagnostic evidence into prioritised opportunities, such as missing product information, inconsistent public details, incomplete FAQs or weak supporting source coverage.

Opportunity scoringEvidence ranking

4. AI Content Optimisation Copilot

Uses verified brand information and diagnostic findings to draft product descriptions, FAQs, structured content and content briefs. Shows supporting evidence and requires human approval.

RAGLLM generationHuman review

5. AI Visibility Experimentation Lab

Manages content variants, repeat prompt assessments and before/after comparisons. Helps assess whether observed changes are consistent. Controlled demo-site experiments can be used if real-brand content cannot be changed.

ExperimentsVersioningMonitoring

6. Autonomous GEO Intelligence Agent

Coordinates the workflow by calling the other engines, checking task state, preparing reports, proposing follow-up actions and pausing for human approval at important steps.

  1. Working GEO Intelligence Platform — one interface to configure and run assessments.
  2. AI Visibility Diagnostic System — measurable visibility, accuracy and source intelligence.
  3. GEO Recommendation Copilot — prioritised, evidence-grounded actions and content drafts.
  4. Experimentation and Monitoring Framework — repeatable runs, variants and comparisons.
  5. Industry-Agnostic Configuration Layer — configurable brand, market, category, competitor and prompt settings.

6. Literature review plan

The literature review is a required parallel workstream. It must synthesise evidence, compare approaches and establish the research gap; it is not simply a list of paper summaries.

Research themes

  1. Generative AI discovery and search behaviour: conversational discovery, answer engines and differences from traditional search.
  2. Generative Engine Optimisation: GEO concepts, proposed optimisation methods, empirical evidence and limitations.
  3. Brand visibility measurement: mention rate, share of voice, recommendation position, query coverage, stability and measurement validity.
  4. Answer quality and factual accuracy: product/entity matching, factual consistency, hallucination and evaluation methods.
  5. Source attribution and trust: citations, source quality, source diversity, provenance and credibility.
  6. NLP and semantic methods: entity recognition, intent classification, information extraction, embeddings and semantic similarity.
  7. RAG and recommendation systems: retrieval, grounding, evidence traceability, content generation and human review.
  8. Agentic AI and workflow orchestration: tool use, multi-step planning, state, reliability, approval gates and evaluation.
  9. Experiment design: repeated measurements, controlled variants, confounding factors, uncertainty and causal inference.
  10. Responsible AI and governance: bias, transparency, privacy, data provenance, copyright/terms and reproducibility.

Research workflow

  1. Write review questions and a search protocol before collecting papers.
  2. Search Google Scholar, academic databases, publisher libraries and institutional library resources.
  3. Prioritise peer-reviewed papers, conference proceedings, foundational books and primary technical documentation.
  4. Use vendor materials for clearly labelled industry context, not as the only evidence for academic claims.
  5. Record search strings, date, source type and inclusion/exclusion rationale.
  6. Extract each source's question, method, data, findings, limitations and relevance.
  7. Synthesise themes and compare evidence; identify contradictions and gaps.
  8. Derive the project's conceptual framework, metric choices and method decisions from the review.

End-to-end example

  1. User chooses a brand, market, competitors, categories and prompt set.
  2. Discovery Engine runs approved prompts against selected AI providers and stores responses, timestamps and provider/model metadata.
  3. Diagnostic Engine extracts brand/product mentions, compares product claims with verified reference data, and analyses citations.
  4. Opportunity Engine identifies possible gaps and ranks them by evidence strength, business relevance and effort.
  5. Copilot retrieves approved brand information and diagnostic evidence to draft recommendations and content variants.
  6. Autonomous Agent coordinates these steps, records state and pauses for human approval before any external publishing or action.
  7. Experimentation Lab runs repeated assessments or controlled variants and reports observed differences and uncertainty.

How the tools fit together (avoid overlap)

  • Databricks is the analytical data-processing foundation; Supabase is the operational application database.
  • GitHub manages code and work; it is not the runtime database.
  • Hugging Face supplies model resources; it does not replace the LLM provider API or our evaluation.
  • pgvector supports semantic retrieval; it does not replace relational data storage or analytical tables.
  • LangGraph coordinates tools; it does not replace the logic inside the six engines.
  • Streamlit is the initial UI; FastAPI is optional and should be introduced only for a clear architectural reason.

Application database vs analytics lakehouse

  • Supabase: brand settings, users/roles if required, saved prompt sets, workflow state, approval status and vector metadata.

Example user story

As a digital marketing manager, I want to select a brand, competitors and a prompt set so that I can run a consistent AI visibility assessment and compare results.

Acceptance criteria: configuration is saved; prompt set has a version; provider/model and timestamp are recorded; raw response is stored; failures are visible; extracted metrics trace back to original responses.

Iteration cadence

  • Planning: select a small, achievable set of tasks for a 2–3 working-day iteration.
  • Async daily log: yesterday's progress, today's task and blocker. If solo, keep a personal log; do not invent stand-ups.
  • Review: show a working feature, test evidence or research finding to teammates/faculty.
  • Retrospective: record what worked, what failed and one process change.
  • Definition of done: code runs, output is checked, data lineage is documented, acceptance criteria are met and GitHub issue is updated.

12. Evaluation strategy and success measures

Visibility and business metrics

  • Brand mention rate: proportion of assessed responses mentioning the brand.
  • Share of mentions: brand mentions relative to the defined competitor set and denominator.
  • Recommendation position: location/order of brand mention using a consistent coding rule.
  • Category/intent coverage: prompt segments where the brand appears.
  • Product accuracy: match of extracted product attributes to verified reference information.
  • Citation analysis: source frequency, diversity and relevance review.
  • Repeatability: variability across repeated prompts/runs.
  • Operational measures: cost per assessment, latency, failure rate and completion rate.

Model and system evaluation

ComponentEvaluation
Entity/product extractionHuman-labelled sample; precision, recall and F1.
Intent classificationBaseline comparison, confusion matrix and macro-F1 if classes are imbalanced.
Semantic matchingHuman-judged relevance sample and agreement review.
Product accuracy checksAttribute-level match/error rate against approved catalogue; track unknown/not-verifiable cases separately.
RAG retrievalRelevance of retrieved evidence, source traceability and whether the generated answer stays within evidence.
Recommendation qualityHuman review for factual support, relevance, actionability, priority and clarity.
Agent workflowTask completion, tool-call correctness, invalid transitions, retry behaviour, approval handling and trace logs.
ExperimentationRepeat runs, variance, confidence intervals where appropriate, version control and confounding limitations.
Visibility scores apply only to the defined prompt sample, providers, geography, language and observation dates. Do not call them market share, universal AI ranking or sales impact. A before/after comparison is observational unless a defensible controlled design supports causal interpretation.

15. Working principles

  • Follow the capstone course outline and CRISP-DM lifecycle; map product work to academic evidence.
  • Build and integrate in short iterations. Each module must have a clear input, output and acceptance criteria.
  • Use simple baselines before complex ML. Do not add a model merely to make the project look advanced.
  • Keep raw, cleaned and derived data separate and traceable.
  • Version prompt sets, brand reference data, content variants and experiment runs.
  • Ground recommendations in evidence and require human approval for external actions.
  • Report sample sizes, uncertainty, provider/model details and limitations.
  • Keep a reproducible GitHub history, decision log and honest contribution record.
  • Build a real integrated prototype first; treat production-scale features as future roadmap.
