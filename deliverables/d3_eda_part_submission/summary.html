D3 EDA: Does Sephora India show up in AI shopping answers?

Team L: Saba Gul, Saurav Kumar, Agnik Majumder  |  Data version data-v0.9: 238 usable of 600 planned answers  |  Generated by: python -m beyondsearch run
Decision supported: Sephora India's marketing and digital team must decide which content and product-feed fixes to make first so that AI shopping assistants name and link Sephora India when shoppers ask for beauty products. Method: 20 shopper questions put to 5 AI engines; plain text rules count which retailers are named and linked. 6 hypotheses were written before any chart (page 2).

The five insights that matter most

  1. Nykaa is named about 3.0 times as often as Sephora India. 32% vs 10% of 222 unbranded answers (53 name only Nykaa, 6 only Sephora; McNemar p<0.001). Nykaa is ahead on 5 of 5 engines and in single fresh chats alone (55% vs 13%).
  2. Sephora appears when shoppers ask where to buy, not what to buy. 83% of 12 retailer questions vs 6% of 210 product questions (Fisher p<0.001). Fix product-answer visibility first.
  3. Engines differ. Sephora is named in 22% of ChatGPT, 11% of Perplexity, 9% of Claude, 5% of Gemini, 3% of Google AI Mode answers (chi-square p=0.016). Google AI Mode names Sephora least.
  4. Engines link to Nykaa far more than to Sephora. Of 74 answers with a retailer link, Nykaa is linked in 74% and Sephora in 23%.
  5. One answer is not reliable. Cited sites overlap 0.30 (1 = identical) between repeat runs of the same question (Gemini 0.05), so any monitor needs several runs per question.

How this shapes modelling (D4)

  • Outcome to model: Sephora named (yes/no) per answer. About 10% are positive, so use stratified folds and report precision-recall, not accuracy.
  • Inputs: question type, category, engine, collection method, early position in the answer.
  • All runs of one prompt stay in the same train or test fold, because repeat runs of a prompt are not independent.
  • Only a few retailer-question answers exist, so model product questions first and add where-to-buy when more data arrives.

Representativeness and bias checks

Engine mix: Gemini 29%, ChatGPT 24%, Claude 24%, Google AI Mode 14%, Perplexity 9% of the 238 answers (plan: popularity-weighted). Collection method (141 batched chat, 57 single chat, 40 api) is kept as a variable and tested instead of excluding rows: effect on Sephora mentions none detected (chi-square p=0.67; inside ChatGPT alone p=1.0). Collector effect: none detected (p=0.11).

Limits: Part submission: the analysis is re-run when the data is updated. Collected with our own accounts; AI answers change with model versions.  •  Engine mix differs from the popularity weights in the plan; the engine and category cells are small, so p-values there are indicative.  •  The question-type test rests on a small number of retailer-question answers.  •  Rules count the retailer name, not the quality of the recommendation. A brand sold at Sephora is not counted as Sephora.  •  Product-only answers rarely name a retailer, so mention rates are low for every retailer. Compare retailers, not absolute levels.  •  Gemini API answers and batched chats were collected differently from single chats; the method check shows no significant effect.  •  No sales or traffic data: we cannot say what a mention is worth in rupees.
